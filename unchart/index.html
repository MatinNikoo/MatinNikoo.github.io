Chart to data, in your browser

Get the numbers back out of a chart.

Paste a screenshot, drop an image or open a PDF. Unchart reads the axis labels, fits the scale and traces every line, bar, slice and dot into a table, with an error bar on each number. When it isn't sure it says so. Then fix any point, redraw the chart the way you want and take it to Excel. Nothing leaves your browser.

01 ReadRead the labels
It finds the small glyphs itself and reads them with Tesseract, then reads every number label again with PP-OCR. Where the two disagree, the one that lines up with the other labels wins.

    
02 FitFit the axes
A line fit that ignores misread labels sets the scale. A lost minus sign or a dropped decimal point gets fixed only when another label proves it.

    
03 TraceTrace the data
Each color becomes a series. Lines snap to the data's x grid, bars are measured to a fraction of a pixel and pie slices are read from their angles.

    
04 CheckCheck itself
It reads the chart again on a smaller copy and looks at about thirty signals, like how well the labels lined up. Below a set confidence it tells you it isn't sure.

Read right means the chart type, the number of series and points and the values all came out right, with the average error at most 2% of the axis range. The synthetic set has 318 charts, and 13 with the axis numbers hidden on purpose are left out of its rates.

    
It refuses a lot, and the charts it does answer are mostly right. I aimed for at most 2% wrong but marked reliable. That held on my dev charts and not on the test sets, so on new charts it trusts itself more than I meant it to.

    
Against DePlot on 27 synthetic charts it read 23 right and DePlot 4, at 2.3 s a chart against 93.8 s on a laptop CPU. Those 27 are an easier subset than the full set.

  

  

    
Where it fails

    
Read the limits first

    
    
      
  • Damaged pictures. Clean synthetic charts are read right 79.0% of the time, scans 46.7% and JPEGs 53.1%. Blur hits the tick labels long before the lines.
    • 
      
  • No axis numbers, no scale. Without them it refuses, unless every bar has its value printed on it.
    • 
      
  • Crowded lines. On Our World in Data line charts 51.2% come out right, and their stacked areas almost never do.
    • 
      
  • Not read at all. Box plots, radar charts and shaded bands. It also can't see 3D effects or pictures sized by area.
