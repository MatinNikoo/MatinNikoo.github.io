When a country's internet goes dark

Nobody can run the same country with its internet left on, so there's no A/B test. I built each country a twin out of countries that stayed online, using GitHub's public data on where code gets pushed from each quarter, and measured the gap. Then I spent most of the time trying to break it.

    How the interval is set. Every donor gets the fake event, and so does the country in earlier quarters. The standard error is the larger of the two spreads, and the permutation p is the share of fake gaps at least as big as the real one.

    The floor. With 106 donors the smallest permutation p is 1 in 107. Corrected for testing nine events with Holm, that floor becomes 0.08.

      • It's GitHub's map. Private repos and everything off GitHub are missing, and VPN users count wherever their VPN exits. Some of each drop is activity placed in another country, and I can't say how much. None of it measures what a shutdown costs an economy.
      • Each number covers the whole quarter. Iran's 2026 blackouts came with mass protests after a currency collapse and then US and Israeli strikes from 28 February. Myanmar's and Sudan's came with coups. The crises tab shows a crisis alone can move this by about 20%.
      • The twin is an assumption. For Iran 2026 the answer depends on whether Iran would have joined the 2026 jump, which moves it between 59% and 69%. I don't know why the jump happened.
      • Short shutdowns are invisible one at a time. Kazakhstan, Tanzania, Pakistan and Bangladesh don't show no effect. They show that quarterly data can't see it.
      • Myanmar passing every check that can run is weaker than it sounds. It has four quarters before the coup, so it can't be backdated, its time placebos are empty and its conformal p sits at the floor of 0.167.
      • Nobody independent has checked this yet.
